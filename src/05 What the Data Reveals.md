# What the Data Reveals: Data Design seen with an Ethical Lens and through Structuralist Methods

## **Defense of the User:**

The usage of TPC in this data design app is especially emphasized with identifying variables such as **race and sex**, making it heavily applicable towards demographic work.

Even further, through our ***Stopped and Searched*** page, we can compare the race and sex with how far do traffic stop situations go. What demographics are having their vehicle searched? What demographics are having their body's searched?

Specifically, in the defense of the user, this data design app opens up easy access to previously put away data, emphasizing an open data policy we suggested in our scenario. The pages ***Hidden in Darkness*** and ***Punished Unequally*** show specifics in cases, giving access to information about date respective stops and stop reasoning through a racialized lens.  

The information that this data design app does not only provide the public with knowledge, but increases transparency and encourages actions to keep government services **accountable.**


## **Collectivization:** 

Applying collectivization and an open data policy with this data design app can provide not just legal professionals with information detailing their client's case but can allow academics to utilize comparative information and visuals to think critically on issues regarding race, police force, etc. 

This being done without *FOIA* requests for information that can often take months at a time with no guarantee of un-redacted information would be a godsend for those scouring for information on traffic stops.

Hard numbers drawn from coding such as the **48.8%** of Black drivers that are stopped vs **40.8%** of White drivers allows for speculation and thoughts that can provoke a call for investigation or even policy change. 

Furthermore, the data shows what might not be a favorable speculative stance, such as the testing of the *Veil of Darkness*.

Data from ***Punished Unequally*** also generate questions through the comparison of census data and who gets cited for more violations during stops. Black drivers specifically who show that they are **4x** more likely to be arrested despite population size suggest disproportionate policing. 

One last thing that may help investigators and journalists uncover more information beyond our statistics is the fact that quotas are discussed. Despite police quotas being illegal within NC, the question itself drums up discussion about ulterior motives behind arrests.

Materialization allows for the discussion on convenience, why does the bureau of investigation for NC only offer information from 2020-2025? Where is 2016-2020?

For ***Stopped and Searched***, data is cleaned and presented visually with audience accessibility in mind, answering direct questions about racial statistics in traffic stops. 

In both ***Hidden in Darkness** and ***Punished Unequally***, we are given the chance to look at information that stem from typical questions such as "How does time affect traffic stops?" and "What kinds of traffic stops are there? What are possible motivations for stopping people?"

By continuing to work on this Data Design App with perhaps more hands on deck and funding, this project could easily achieve D'Ignazio and Klein's Principle 7; Make Labor Visible.

A data design app that is not only raising questions on missing information but providing comparative statistics is a result of data feminism in action. Accessibility and questions being answered easily through an open data policy.

## What's Next?
### Policy Proposals?
Currently, police officers are free to conduct **warrantless** searches of vehicles as long as they are deemed to have probable cause. Under federal law these searches are most frequently evaluated via the **reasonable person** standard, in which it is question if a prudent person were to believe a crime has occurred. This gives officers free reign to exercise warrantless searches based on arbitrary distinctions, completely contingent on what **prudent** may mean in certain localities or police departements. One suggestion may be for a more codified and rigid legal definitions of probable cause.

Another proposal may be to move away from the **investigatory stop**. Traffic stops are intended to promote traffic safety, not to investigate crime. It is clear from our findings that the application of traffic stops in trying to curtail non-traffic related crime has resulted in unfair biases.

Lastly, police quotas must be abolished federally. Thankfully they have been ruled illegall in NC, but this does not prove that they may still exist illegally within smaller departments. Any evidence of these quotas existing, either formally or informally, muse be deemed illegall.
